ilai: Meals From Your Kitchen — Privacy policy

Effective 28 October 2026. Contact: planmealwithilai@gmail.com.

ilai helps a household log what it cooks and eats. This page says what the app stores, why, who can see it and how to delete it.

What we store. When you sign in with Google, we keep your Google account’s name, email address, the link to your Google profile picture and a user ID, so you can sign in again. The app doesn’t show or use the picture. When you sign in with Apple, we keep the name and email address your Apple ID shares (or Apple’s private-relay address, if you hide your email), a user ID and Apple’s ID for you. Apple shares your name only the first time you sign in, and only if you allow it. In your kitchen we store what you enter: the name you go by, the kitchen’s name, its cuisines, diet and meal times, the name you give Little Idly, the meals you log (the date, the meal, the dishes, who logged or changed it, and when), dishes your kitchen adds, and invites (a code and the name of the person you invite). If your kitchen plans ahead, we also store the meal plans your kitchen makes (which meals, which dishes, who planned or changed them, and when) and how many days ahead it plans. For each meal we keep the suggestions ilai showed and why, as a history, so ilai can be checked and improved. We store when each person looked at a meal’s suggestions in the planner and how often, and the reason chosen when someone taps 👎. When a planned meal is logged, we note whether it followed the plan and what changed. We keep a compact copy of your kitchen’s last 400 days of meals, and of which new dishes it tried or skipped, which ilai uses to make suggestions; the dish pairs your kitchen has blocked or allowed, with who and when; and when someone finishes planning a few meals (who, how many, and when). We store the evening-reminder time and each person’s on or off, and a record of each notification we send (who it went to, its text and when). For each phone you sign in on we store a push token, so ilai can send the evening reminder and planning notices. Most of these records refer to people by user ID, not by name; a notification’s text can include a name, such as “Amma planned 15 meals”. We store your kitchen’s time zone so “today” is right; we don’t collect your precise location (the usage and crash reports below carry an approximate one). The Firebase SDK in the app sends a Firebase installation ID and an app-instance ID with its requests to our server. The app checks that requests come from the real app on a real phone: on Android with Google Play Integrity, on iPhone with Apple’s App Attest.

How we use it. Each kitchen’s suggestions come only from its own meals. Nothing from one kitchen appears in another’s suggestions. We look at meal logs to improve ilai’s suggestions for everyone: we use them to tune the settings every kitchen shares, never to show one kitchen’s meals to another.

Usage and crash reports. To see what works and to fix crashes, ilai sends usage reports (which screens you open and actions such as logging a meal or choosing a plan, with counts and the meal of the day, never what you typed or dish names) and crash reports (what the app was doing when it crashed, the phone model and system version) to Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics. They carry your ilai user ID so one person on two phones counts once and a crash you tell us about can be found; never your name or email. They also hold a few traits of your kitchen (how many people, its diet and main cuisine) and of your phone (notification permission, text size, light or dark). Google Analytics also records the basics it collects itself: first open, sessions, the app version, phone model, system version and language, and an approximate location (country or city) that Google works out from your connection. We don’t collect your phone’s advertising ID. You can turn this off on each phone in Kitchen › Settings › Share usage and crash reports. Usage reports are kept for 14 months, crash reports for 90 days.

What we don’t do. No ads, and no tracking across apps or websites. We don’t sell or share your data. We don’t read your contacts, photos, precise location or other apps. The app sends notifications only for planning: the evening reminder and notices when someone in your kitchen plans or changes a meal. You can turn the evening reminder off for yourself in the app, or turn notifications off in your phone’s settings.

Who can see it. The people in your kitchen see your name and the kitchen’s meals. Nobody else can through the app: the database only lets members of a kitchen read it, and the suggestion history is kept on our server, which the app doesn’t read. The one exception is us: we look at meal logs, as described under How we use it. Notifications are delivered through Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging (and, on iPhone, Apple’s notification service), so their text passes through Google (and Apple). The data is stored with Google Firebase (Google Cloud, Mumbai region), which processes it for us as a service provider. Usage and crash reports are processed by Google (Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics), also as our service provider, and may be stored outside India. We would disclose data only if the law required it.

On your phone. The app keeps a copy of your kitchen on the phone so it works offline. It isn’t included in phone backups. Deleting your account clears it.

How long we keep it. As long as you’re in a kitchen. If you leave, your name stays in that kitchen’s list of people who left, next to the meals you logged. If you’re the last person to leave, the kitchen and all its meals are deleted. Suggestions, views, 👎 reasons and the other plan details are kept with the meal they belong to and are deleted with it, when the kitchen is deleted (the last person leaves or deletes their account). The 400-day copy, pair choices and planning records are kept with the kitchen and deleted with it. A notification record is deleted a few days after it is sent, and at once when you leave your kitchen or delete your account. A phone’s push token is deleted when you sign out, leave your kitchen or delete your account. Invites expire after 7 days and are deleted 7 days later. When you delete your account, a short marker with your user ID is kept for a day or two, so a phone still signed in can’t put you back in a kitchen, and then deleted.

Deleting your account. In the app: Kitchen → Settings → Delete my account. If you have no kitchen (you never set one up, or you just left one), it’s at the bottom of the Start screen and of the screen after leaving. You confirm with Google or Apple, whichever you signed in with; if you signed in with Apple, ilai also revokes its Sign in with Apple access. This deletes your sign-in record and erases your name everywhere; meals you logged stay for the others, shown as “by a past member”. It deletes the invites you used and the open invites you made. An invite you made that someone else used stays until it is deleted automatically (up to 14 days after it was made); it holds your user ID but not your name. If you’re the last person in your kitchen, deleting your account deletes the kitchen and all its meals too. If you can’t open the app, email planmealwithilai@gmail.com from the address of your Google or Apple account and we’ll delete it within 30 days. Deleting your account also clears your user ID from new usage and crash reports; reports already sent expire as described under Usage and crash reports. Deleted data leaves Google’s backups on Google’s own schedule.

Age. ilai is for people 13 and over. It isn’t directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their data. If you think a child under 13 is using it, email planmealwithilai@gmail.com and we’ll delete that account.

Changes. If this policy changes we’ll update this page and its date.
